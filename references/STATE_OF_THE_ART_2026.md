# State of the art snapshot - 1 September 2026

PICEE-PA must be positioned as a **public-administration application profile proposal**, not as the first generic agent-governance stack.

## Closest prior art

- EU AI Act / Regulation (EU) 2026/1744: risk, transparency, governance and updated high-risk application dates.
- Italian Law 132/2025 and 2026 AgID draft guidelines: public-sector AI principles, lifecycle, architecture and procurement.
- Government of Canada Agentic AI Guide: bounded autonomy, recoverability, unique agent identities, human checkpoints, kill switch and durable logging.
- NIST software/AI agent identity and authorization concept work: identity, authorization, auditing and non-repudiation.
- Microsoft Agent Governance Toolkit / Agent Control Specification: deterministic fail-closed policy enforcement, agent identity/trust, execution controls, approval, sandboxing and audit/evidence.
- OPA/Rego, Cedar and OAuth RAR: reusable technical authorization building blocks.
- W3C PROV: reusable provenance model.
- Data & Society (July/August 2026): human-in-the-loop is insufficient without knowledge, observability, meaningful control and timely intervention.
- Frontiers (accepted 19 August 2026): the “democratic authorization gap” makes explicit the difference between technical permission and democratically/administratively grounded authority.
- Urban Institute (19 August 2026): responsible agentic AI playbook for state and local government.
- Interoperable Europe / SyRCI-IA (26 August 2026): concrete public-sector pattern with AI separated from the transactional application, human supervision, formal security/compliance assessment and reusable integration.

## Defensible PICEE-PA contribution

The proposed contribution is the **per-action bridge between administrative authorization and technical authorization**, expressed as:

- PICEE control chain;
- action classes L0-L3;
- AEE as pre-execution contract;
- AEB as post-execution evidence;
- vendor-neutral mapping to existing enforcement stacks.

This contribution remains a hypothesis until independently implemented and evaluated.
